# Imperial Magus — Narrative Design (second pass)

A narrative designer's pass on the Dee game, written after the franchise scaffold and
before the research pipeline. The first pass ([../../../docs/PROTAGONISTS.md](../../../docs/PROTAGONISTS.md))
established the systems. This pass establishes the *story* — the shape of the life, the
load-bearing relationships, where the drama actually lives, and how the mechanics serve
it rather than sit beside it.

Everything here is design intent. Every factual claim below must be re-verified against
`BIOGRAPHY.md` once it exists; where this document and the biography disagree, the
biography wins.

---

## 1. The shape of the life is not Turka's shape

TurkaGame's spine is three inquisitions: survive two, lose the third. Bruno's spine is
the same shape stretched across Europe. It is tempting to pour Dee into that mold, and it
would be wrong, because the mold doesn't fit — and the way it doesn't fit is the best
thing this game has.

Dee is never destroyed by a tribunal. He is destroyed by **the absence of one**.

The attested sequence:

- **1555.** Under Mary, Dee actually *is* arrested — for casting the queen's nativity —
  examined in the Star Chamber, and cleared. He then, in the game's cruelest historical
  irony, lodges with Bishop Bonner, the era's most feared examiner of heretics. So the
  game gets its one classic inquisition beat early, in Act II, and Dee *survives it*. The
  player learns the Turka grammar: accusation, defense, survival.
- **The middle life.** No tribunal ever comes again. Instead: a slander he cannot shake
  ("the conjurer"), promised pensions that never arrive, a rebuke at Prague, the library
  sacked by a mob while he is abroad — damage that arrives without any process attached
  to it, nothing to answer, no one to out-argue.
- **1604–05.** The inversion, and the game's thesis moment: the old Dee **petitions King
  James to be formally tried** — begs for the tribunal — so that he can be cleared of the
  conjurer label at law, "to be tried and cleared of that horrible and damnable... sclaunder."
  He is refused. The state will not even dignify him with an accusation.

**Design consequence:** the series' comparative historiography gets done *through the
mechanics*. In TurkaGame the tribunal is the thing that destroys you. In Imperial Magus
the tribunal is the thing you cannot get — because a charge can be answered, and a stain
cannot. Act VII should contain, as a real playable choice, the petition to be tried; and
its consequence beat, in every variant, is a refusal. The one gate that never opens.

This also seeds Trithemius's suspicion-as-stain mechanic two games early, which is what a
series is for.

## 2. The eight acts

Chronological, like Turka — the life supplies the structure, per the franchise principle.
Working titles use Dee's own vocabulary where possible.

| Act | Years | Title (working) | The room of the life |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1542–1551 | *The Trellis* | Cambridge; Louvain and Paris; Mercator's globes; the 18-hours-a-day legend (LEGEND-tagged — it is Dee's own self-report). Formation: the player's first capability investments, and the **destiny declaration**. |
| II | 1552–1558 | *The Horoscope* | Service under Mary; the 1555 arrest and Star Chamber examination; Bonner's household. The classic inquisition beat, survivable, teaching the grammar. |
| III | 1558–1570 | *The Election* | Elizabeth's accession; choosing the coronation date; *Propaedeumata Aphoristica*; the *Monas Hieroglyphica* written in twelve days at Antwerp; the Euclid preface. The rise. |
| IV | 1570–1582 | *The Limits* | Mortlake and the library (~4,000 volumes, the largest in England); navigation and the "British Impire"; Frobisher; petitions and half-promises; ACCESS at its zenith while money never arrives. "The Queen's favor is not the Queen's money" is this act's refrain. |
| V | 1581–1583 | *The Glass* | The angelic sessions begin. Barnabas Saul, then Edward Kelley. The shewstone. The epistemic-risk mechanic arrives at full strength, and every use of it is also a trust-Kelley choice. |
| VI | 1583–1589 | *The Crossing* | Łaski; the departure; Prague and Rudolf's rebuke; Třeboň; the 1587 crisis; and — offstage, delayed — the sack of the Mortlake library, whose news reaches the player acts after the choice that caused it. |
| VII | 1589–1605 | *The Petition* | Return to a stripped house; the Manchester wardenship among hostile fellows; poverty; the petition to James to be tried, refused. The inverted inquisition. |
| VIII | 1605–1609 | *The Catalogue* | Mortlake again; selling books to eat; his daughter Katherine keeping the house; the quiet end. The ending computes here, and the journal renders. |

Eight acts, ~40 choices, 8–12 minutes a run — the Turka budget, deliberately, because the
replay length is what makes divergence mean anything.

## 3. The load-bearing relationship: Kelley, and the rule of no ground truth

Dee could not see or hear the angels himself. Everything — every name, every table, every
instruction, all of Enochian — arrives through Edward Kelley: a man with a documented
history of forgery, and (LEGEND — the cropped-ears story is later tradition, not
established record) a possibly mutilated one.

**The rule: the game never tells the player whether the angels are real.** No consequence
text, no epilogue, no ending — not one line of the game's own voice — ever settles it.
Consequences report what happened *socially and materially*: who believed, who paid, who
withdrew, what was written down. The three hidden states move
(`angelic_authority`, `scholarly_credibility`, `political_utility`) and the world responds
to them, but the metaphysical question is left exactly where the historical record leaves
it. A player who finishes convinced Kelley was a fraud and a player who finishes
convinced the sessions were genuine should both be able to point at their playthrough as
evidence. That is the epistemic-risk mechanic doing its real work: the *player* is put in
Dee's chair, not told how to feel about having sat in it.

Every "should I ask?" choice (accept / verify / delay / reject / publicize / conceal) is
therefore double-written: it is about the message, and it is about the man delivering it.
The verify option is the most expensive and the least conclusive — historically true, and
mechanically the point.

## 4. The library is the delayed-consequence engine

The Mortlake library is the game's material soul, and its fate is the strongest gate in
the design:

- Acts III–IV build it, choice by choice — each acquisition a small decision with a cost.
- The Act VI departure choice (go with Łaski, or refuse) is made *without the game
  foreshadowing what it costs*. Silent gate, per the Turka convention.
- The news of the sack arrives late in Act VI or in Act VII, at a distance, secondhand —
  the player, like Dee, hears about it from across a continent and can do nothing.
- What survives of the library then varies by earlier choices (what was lent, hidden,
  duplicated, carried abroad), and feeds the ending.

**And the ending journal is the library catalogue.** Instead of Turka's chronological
receipt — a diagnosed weakness — Dee's journal renders as *"The Catalogue of the Library
of Doctor Dee"*: the run's choices grouped as shelved works under thematic headings —
**What He Asked. Whom He Told. What He Kept. What Was Promised. What Was Scattered.** The
franchise's "thematic journal" fix, realized diegetically.

## 5. The interface conceit: marginalia

Dee was the most famous annotator in the history of English books — his surviving
volumes are studied *for* their marginal notes. The interface adopts this wholesale:

- Scene text is the page; **consequence text is a marginal annotation** in a second ink,
  written slightly aslant. The consequence beat — the loop's most important screen —
  becomes Dee's own hand commenting on his life as it happens.
- The grounding badge renders as a cataloguer's mark. ATTESTED in a steady hand;
  PLAUSIBLE-GAP annotated *"the record is silent here"*; LEGEND *"so it was later told"*;
  COUNTERFACTUAL *"so it might have been"* (see §7).
- Capability-gated blue choices render as **underlinings** — the passage only this Dee
  would have marked.
- Palette ramp (per the FOUNDER.md technique): London ink-and-vellum (Acts I–IV, warm
  paper, iron-gall brown, red rule lines) → the Glass (Act V, the palette cools, more
  black, one point of crystal light) → Prague gold-and-shadow (Act VI, Rudolfine dark
  ground, gold leaf) → the stripped house (Acts VII–VIII, the warm paper returns but
  faded, wider margins, emptier page). The page itself ages with the man.

## 6. The verbs, and the panel

Seven verbs — **Calculate, Predict, Experiment, Invoke, Navigate, Map, Persuade** — as
quiet scores in the persistent panel, Turka-style: peripheral, never demanding attention.
Gates read them (`capability_gate: {navigate: 3}`); blue choices are their visible face.

The destiny declaration (Act I, at Louvain): the player names what Dee is trying to
become — **"One perfect science"** (the Monas path), **"The British Impire"** (the
navigation path), or **"The conversation of angels"** (the invocation path — selectable
this early only as an inclination, not knowledge). This is the Providence hook: it biases
which optional scenes surface in Acts III–VI, probabilistically, never deterministically.
The world notices what you are trying to become; it does not hand it to you.

## 7. The Ottoman route, and a fourth grounding tag

The FOUNDER.md conditional-patron mechanic, transposed. The player can spend Acts III–VI
accumulating the makings of an eastward turn: Arabic and Hebrew texts in the library, the
*Book of Soyga* thread (its Uriel/Michael lore, per Melvin-Koushki, deriving from the
Bunian-Bistamian corpus popular in Ottoman courtly circles), merchant contacts of the
Turkey trade, astrological-talismanic credibility of the kind Murad III — Sufi initiate,
oneiromancer, self-fashioned prophet-king on the strength of his own spirit
communications — actually patronized.

Then, late, the opportunity surfaces — *only if the player has become the kind of scholar
that court would want*. The default resolution is the near-miss, framed as historically
normal, exactly per the Ulugh Beg pattern: the introduction exists, the interest is real,
and the life runs out of room.

But here the sequel must go one honest step beyond TurkaGame. Ulugh Beg was a real
would-be patron; Murad III is a *counterfactual* one — the entire route is
Melvin-Koushki's explicit thought experiment, contrary to the record, not merely
unattested by it. INVENTED-COMPATIBLE is therefore the wrong tag. The route gets a fourth
mark:

> **COUNTERFACTUAL** — contrary to the documented record, licensed by a named scholarly
> counterfactual, cited. Rendered in the margin as *"so it might have been."*

The rare, earned full ending — Dee at Constantinople — plays under that mark from its
first scene, and its epilogue says plainly what the record actually holds: he never went;
he died at Mortlake; this is the road not taken, played. The game gets to have its
alternate history *and* its integrity, because the badge system was built for exactly
this distinction.

## 8. The 1587 problem — flagged for the user, not decided here

The angelic sessions' most notorious attested episode: in 1587 at Třeboň, Kelley
delivered an angelic instruction that he and Dee hold all things in common — including
their wives — and Dee, after documented anguish, consented; his own diary records the
covenant and, later, its bitter aftermath, shortly before the two men parted forever. It
is ATTESTED, it is the single most extreme test of the accept/verify/reject mechanic in
the entire life (the point where obedience to the glass costs more than career or money),
and it is also the kind of material that changes a game's register and rating.

Options: (a) include as a playable choice, handled at the diary's own distance — the
scene is the anguish and the decision, nothing more explicit than the historical record
itself; (b) allude — the crisis is present as the reason the partnership breaks, but the
player does not choose; (c) omit the content entirely and let the partnership end on the
documented quarrels alone. **This is the user's call, asked before the choice graph is
authored.** The design's recommendation is (a): it is the era's most famous demonstration
that the epistemic-risk mechanic was never a game to the people living it, and the
diary-distance treatment keeps it a scene about belief and cost, not spectacle.

## 9. Endings (sketch)

Computed from final state; the historical outcome is one among several, unprivileged.

1. **The Servant of the Glass** — the historical shape: the sessions pursued to the end,
   the Continent, the return, the stripped library, death at Mortlake with Katherine
   keeping the house. High `angelic_authority`, spent `scholarly_credibility`.
2. **The Queen's Philosopher** — never crosses; the Act VI gate refused. Astrologer,
   adviser, mathematician; the library survives; the angels remain a private matter or
   are never sought. The safest life, and the game does not pretend it is the largest.
3. **The Imperial Geographer** — the navigation destiny carried through; the "British
   Impire" texts and the voyages as legacy; the occult work stays marginal. High
   `political_utility`.
4. **The Mathematical Philosopher** — the Euclid-preface line: influence through the
   mathematicians and mechanicians who read him, the conjurer slander outlived by the
   textbook. Highest `scholarly_credibility` path.
5. **The Rudolfine Alchemist** — remains in Bohemia; Kelley's path shadowing his;
   patronage held or lost by the Act VI–VII choices.
6. **The Conjurer of Mortlake** — the worst road: credibility and utility both spent,
   the slander total, the petition (if made) refused in its cruelest variant.
7. **The Sultan's Astronomer** — COUNTERFACTUAL, rare, earned; §7's route completed.
   The epilogue names the real record while the scene plays the might-have-been.

Every ending closes with the Catalogue (§4), and — matching TurkaGame's two-century coda
(the *Zīj-i Shāhjahānī* beat) — a reception coda: the diaries surfacing in a fishmonger's
hands and a chest's false bottom, Casaubon's 1659 edition printing the conversations to
damn them and preserving them forever, Elias Ashmole collecting the relics; the player's
particular Dee shading which afterlife the coda emphasizes.

## 10. What the research pipeline must now produce

In order:

1. **`BIOGRAPHY.md`** — the canonical, claim-tagged life (ATTESTED / PLAUSIBLE-GAP /
   LEGEND / COUNTERFACTUAL / CONTEXT), chronological, with a "for game design" section
   naming NPCs (Kelley, Jane Dee, Łaski, Rudolf II, Burghley, Walsingham, Bonner,
   Katherine Dee, Ashmole as afterlife) and open gaps. Built from Melvin-Koushki 2021
   (on disk, with a synthesis note in TurkaGame), Shumaker, Asprem-on-Rudd, Davies,
   Yates — plus clearly-tagged CONTEXT from standard Dee scholarship, with the honesty
   note that the major Dee monographs are not currently on disk.
2. **`timeline.json`** — dated events with category, grounding, source; the scene-writing
   reference layer, per the Turka pattern.
3. **`WRITING_GUIDE.md`** — the house rule specialized to Dee: real names, real texts
   (*Monas*, *Propaedeumata*, the *General and Rare Memorials*, the Euclid preface, the
   *Libri Mysteriorum*), the marginalia conceit's prose register, and the ~10–20 word
   option-length anchor inherited numerically.
4. **`CHOICES.md`** then **`choices.json`** — the 40, only after 1–3 exist.
