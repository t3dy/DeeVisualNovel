- **ch. 1, Pythagorean Number Symbolism, Alchemy, and the Disciplina Noua** (Pythagorean Number Symbolism, Alchemy, and the Disciplina Noua of John Dee's Monas Hieroglyphica, pp. 1–23)

### William H. Sherman

- **ch. 1, Politics in the Margins: The Careers of John Dee** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 15–56)
  - it replaces the isolated magus with a reader-writer operating inside Elizabethan systems of access, dependence, counsel, and reputation
- **ch. 2, A Living Library: The Bibliotheca Mortlacensis Revisited** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 56–80)
  - it turns Dee's library from a static monument of omnivorous learning into a social and intellectual practice
- **ch. 3, Reading: Modern Theory and Early Modern Practice** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 80–116)
  - it gives Dee studies a method for treating reading marks as evidence rather than ornament
- **ch. 4, Dee's Marginalia** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 116–204)
  - it makes the marks in Dee's books central evidence for his intellectual and political projects
- **ch. 5, Dee's Political Science: An Introduction to the Manuscript Writings** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 204–216)
  - it restores Dee's manuscript political writings to the history of Elizabethan political culture
- **ch. 6, Brytannicae Reipublicae Synopsis: A Reader's Guide to the Elizabethan Commonwealth** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 216–277)
  - it treats Dee as a systematic reader of the commonwealth rather than as a marginal occult adviser
- **ch. 7, This British Discovery and Recovery Enterprise: Dee and England's Maritime Empire** (John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance, pp. 277–394)
  - it places Dee inside Elizabethan empire-building, showing how scholarship became a tool of territorial and maritime claim-making

## 5. How to search the corpus directly

```python
import sqlite3
c = sqlite3.connect("file:E:/pdf/renaissance magic/Dee/DeeChunks/dee_chunks.sqlite?mode=ro", uri=True)
for r in c.execute("SELECT c.pages, d.title, snippet(chunk_fts, 0, '**', '**', '...', 24) FROM chunk_fts f JOIN chunks c ON c.chunk_id=f.rowid JOIN documents d ON d.doc_id=c.doc_id WHERE chunk_fts MATCH ? LIMIT 10", ("Murphyn",)):
    print(r)
```
