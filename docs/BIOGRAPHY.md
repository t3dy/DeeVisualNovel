# BIOGRAPHY.md — John Dee (1527–1608/09)

The canonical, citation-grounded biography for *Imperial Magus* — the file scenes are
drawn **from**, superseding looser design prose wherever they disagree. Built on the
TurkaGame pattern (`../../../../TurkaGame/docs/BIOGRAPHY.md`).

**Revised 2026-08-31**, second pass: the Dee scholarship was located on disk and the
claims below were checked against it. Most of what this file previously tagged CONTEXT
("standard scholarship, not on disk") is now **ATTESTED with page ranges**.

## Grounding tags

- **ATTESTED** — supported by a named source in the corpus, cited. The game may state it.
- **CONTEXT** — general history the corpus assumes rather than argues. Usable, but never
  leaned on harder than an ATTESTED claim.
- **DISPUTED** — the scholarship actively disagrees. **The game writes the dispute, never
  the resolution.** See [HISTORIOGRAPHICAL_ISSUES.md](HISTORIOGRAPHICAL_ISSUES.md).
- **LEGEND** — attested as later tradition, or as Dee's own self-fashioning, not as event.
  Playable as what people believed, including what Dee wanted believed about himself.
- **COMPARATIVE** — a comparison a source itself draws. Never a claim of contact.
- **COUNTERFACTUAL** — contrary to the record, licensed by a named scholarly
  counterfactual, cited. Exactly one item: the Ottoman route.

## Vitals

- **Born** 13 July 1527, London. Father Rowland Dee, mercer and gentleman sewer at
  Henry VIII's court. (ATTESTED — Parry 23–27; Harkness 26–28.) Welsh royal descent from
  Rhodri the Great is **Dee's own claim** and mattered to his self-fashioning, especially
  once he needed British-antiquity credentials for the imperial writings. (LEGEND — Parry
  23–27.)
- **Died** at Mortlake, December 1608 or early 1609. The parish register is lost and the
  date is genuinely uncertain; the game may honestly say the record goes quiet.
  (ATTESTED — Harkness 249–267; Parry 273–322.)
- **Occupations**: consulting astrologer and adviser; technical adviser on navigation;
  from 1595, Warden of Christ's College, Manchester. Never the settled endowed position he
  sought — Sherman's point that the *failed* offices are the career. (ATTESTED — Sherman
  ch. 1, 15–56; Parry 258–273.)
- **The project**: a universal science joining mathematics, natural philosophy, astrology,
  alchemy, cabala and — from 1581 — direct angelic instruction, under eschatological
  pressure. (ATTESTED — Harkness, throughout; Szőnyi ch. 6–7, 155–241.)
- **The series' charter**: "Ibn Turka is best approached as a Timurid Dr. Dee — or Dee
  best approached as an Elizabethan Dr. Littleturk." Both pursued mathematical, magical,
  kabbalistic/lettrist, alchemical and anagogic unification in service of a millenarian
  one-world empire; both were smeared in their own day; both were more influential after
  death. (COMPARATIVE — M-K 2021, direct quotation of the framing.)

## Act I — Formation (1542–1551)

- **1542**, St John's College, Cambridge, at fifteen; trained in logic, mathematics and
  the humanist disciplines. (ATTESTED — Parry 29–35; Harkness 26–30.)
- **1546**, founding fellow of Trinity at its creation; taught logic and sophistry in a
  university culture already dependent on patronage — Parry's framing, and the first
  instance of the pattern that governs the whole life. (ATTESTED — Parry 31–35.)
- **Eighteen hours a day** of study, four asleep, two for meals. (LEGEND — Dee's own
  report in the *Compendious Rehearsal*. Play it as the story he told about himself.)
- **The *Pax* scarab** — a flying beetle engineered for a Trinity production of
  Aristophanes' *Peace*, after which, by his account, the whispering began. (LEGEND — the
  event is reported; the *causal* claim that his reputation began here is Dee's own.
  Parry's ch. 6 argument, that reputation is made socially and deliberately, cuts against
  taking Dee's origin story at face value.)
- **1548–1550, Louvain** — a major centre of mathematical, astronomical and instrument
  culture; **Gemma Frisius** and **Gerard Mercator**. Parry's chapter 2 makes the point
  that matters for the game: this is where mathematics and astrology become *practical
  technologies of service*. He returns with instruments and globes. (ATTESTED — Parry
  35–48; Harkness 28–30.)
- **1550, Paris** — lectures on Euclid; his continental reputation as a mathematical
  teacher is established here. (ATTESTED — Harkness 28–30; Szőnyi.) The overflowing hall
  and the declined readership are Dee's own account. (LEGEND.)

## Act II — Suspicion (1552–1558)

- Service in the households of Pembroke and then the Duke of Northumberland. (CONTEXT.)
- Under Mary, nativities cast for the Queen, Philip, and Princess Elizabeth, with
  Elizabeth's household's encouragement. (ATTESTED — Parry 48–58.)
- **1555: arrested.** Astrological and prognosticating expertise draws suspicion; charges
  around calculing and conjuring; imprisonment and examination. Parry's chapter 3 is
  titled *Conjuring the Future* and its argument is exactly the game's: magical expertise
  is simultaneously **useful and incriminating**. (ATTESTED — Parry 48–58.)
- **George Ferrers** as accuser and **Secretary John Bourne** as interrogator. (ATTESTED,
  thinly for Ferrers — 7 corpus hits; Bourne 42. See
  [LEAD_VERIFICATION.md](LEAD_VERIFICATION.md).) The story that Ferrers' children were
  struck blind and died, feeding rumour of magical revenge, is only weakly present in the
  corpus (2 hits) — **do not assert it**; it may be used as *rumour reported by others*.
- Cleared of treason, held on religion, and remanded to **Bishop Edmund Bonner** — whose
  household he then joined. (ATTESTED — Parry 48–58; the association's *nature* is
  **DISPUTED**: collaboration, survival, or conviction. Write the dispute.)
- The episode reaches print in **Foxe's *Acts and Monuments***, which fixes an image
  early and publicly. (ATTESTED — 36 corpus hits.)

**Design note.** This is the game's one classic, survivable inquisition. Every later
injury arrives without process attached. See
[NARRATIVE_DESIGN.md](NARRATIVE_DESIGN.md) §1.

## Act III — The Election (1558–1570)

- **1558–1559**, consulted around Elizabeth's accession; the election of the coronation
  date. (ATTESTED — Parry 48–58; 34 corpus hits for the coronation.)
- **1558, *Propaedeumata Aphoristica*** — astrology rebuilt as a physics of rays.
  (ATTESTED — Harkness 98–102; Szőnyi.) Szőnyi's ch. 6 insists this printed natural
  philosophy is *already* symbolic magic — which is why Act V is not a rupture.
- **1564, *Monas Hieroglyphica***, written at Antwerp in the house of the royal
  typographer **Willem Silvius**. (ATTESTED — Harkness 76–90; Clucas, *Ambix* 64.2, on
  Silvius and the diagrammatic/typographical presentation; 32 corpus hits for Silvius.)
  **DISPUTED, and the game's best use of a dispute**: narrowly alchemical (Parry 68–81),
  geometrical cabala (Walton), or a *disciplina noua* grounded in Pythagorean and
  Trithemian number symbolism, broader than alchemy (Clucas). Its continental afterlife
  through Khunrath, Libavius, the *Theatrum Chemicum* and into Rosicrucianism is
  established (Forshaw). Dedicated to **Maximilian II** (ATTESTED — 104 hits).
  **Melvin-Koushki's hinge**: Ibn Turka's Ṭahawī Circle is "the Islamic answer to Dee's
  Hieroglyphic Monad." (COMPARATIVE — M-K 2021.)
- **1570, the "Mathematicall Praeface"** to Billingsley's English Euclid — the taxonomy of
  the mathematical arts, addressed to craftsmen and mechanicians outside the universities.
  (ATTESTED — Sherman.) **Dee complains in it of Murphyn's intensifying slanders**
  (Parry 100–105) — the earliest hard date for the stain, and the reason `d20` sits here
  rather than c.1580.

## Act IV — The Limits (1570–1582)

- **The Mortlake library**, built through the 1560s–70s: the greatest private collection
  in England, and — Sherman's argument — a *living working institution* of acquisition,
  annotation, consultation, lending and loss, not a monument. (ATTESTED — Sherman ch. 2,
  56–80; the marginalia as central evidence, ch. 4, 116–204.)
- **1570, *Brytannicae Reipublicae Synopsis*** — the commonwealth reading: offices, navy,
  fisheries, reform. (ATTESTED — Sherman ch. 6, 216–277.)
- **1576–1577, *General and Rare Memorials*** and the imperial programme; **Ramusio**
  annotated during the **Frobisher** voyages; **Arthur and Madoc** as prior title against
  the papal donation. (ATTESTED — Sherman ch. 7, 277–394; 19 hits Ramusio, 27 Madoc.)
  **DISPUTED**: real maritime policy built from reading and legal argument (Sherman) or
  occult ambition in political code — "more is hid, than uttered" (Parry 127–138).
- **Rivals.** **John Prestall**, who prospers on bolder alchemical promises (ATTESTED —
  Parry 92–114; 23 hits). **Vincent Murphyn**, forger, who runs a slander campaign from
  the 1560s, forges letters in Dee's name, and damages his prospects for appointments over
  two decades (ATTESTED — Parry 87–109; 26 hits). Parry's thesis: **reputation is a
  historical force**, manufactured and weaponised (ch. 6, 81–94; ch. 8, 103–114).
- **1582, calendar reform** — Dee gives the mathematics; the bishops refuse. A problem
  simultaneously political, religious, mathematical and confessional. (ATTESTED — Parry
  170–183; Harkness 155–159; 147 hits.)
- **1578**, marries **Jane Fromond**. (CONTEXT for the marriage; Jane is heavily present
  in the diaries — see Act VI.)

## Act V — The Glass (1581–1583)

- **22 December 1581: the first surviving angelic action.** (ATTESTED — Harkness 35–42;
  Whitby's edition covers 22 Dec 1581 – 23 May 1583.)
- **The motive is not credulity.** Harkness's argument, the title of her book: cabala,
  alchemy, and *the end of Nature* — the Book of Nature is decaying and an Adamic
  exegetical key is needed before the eschaton. (ATTESTED — Harkness throughout;
  Håkansson ch. 5, 240–332, integrating ritual magic, theology and eschatology.)
- **Barnabas Saul**, the first scryer, who fails and recants. (ATTESTED — 119 hits.)
- **March 1582: Edward Kelley** enters, under the name **Talbot**. (ATTESTED — Harkness
  35–40; Parry 55–58; 74 hits for Talbot.) His forgery/coining conviction: CONTEXT. The
  **cropped ears**: LEGEND.
- **Jane Dee's warnings** that the man was a counterfeit. (ATTESTED in kind — the diaries
  record household conflict around Kelley extensively; 251 hits on the query. The specific
  phrase "marvellous rage" should be checked in Fenton before quoting.)
- **The *Book of Soyga***, put to the angels; Uriel defers, Michael is named as its
  expounder. (ATTESTED — 89 hits.) Melvin-Koushki argues its lore derives from the
  **Bunian-Bistamian** magical corpus popular in Ottoman courtly circles — the eastward
  thread present in the very first sessions. (ATTESTED as M-K's argument — M-K 2021.)
- **1582–1583: the Heptarchic system** takes shape; the tables, the seals, the *Sigillum*.
  (ATTESTED — Harkness 40–44; Håkansson; 167 hits.)
- **Kelley's sincerity is DISPUTED and the corpus does not adjudicate it.** Neither does
  the game. See [HISTORIOGRAPHICAL_ISSUES.md](HISTORIOGRAPHICAL_ISSUES.md) §8.

## Act VI — The Crossing (1583–1589)

- **September 1583**: departure with **Albert Łaski**. Parry reads the continental turn
  through prophecy, kingship and apocalyptic expectation — Łaski's dynastic ambition and
  Dee's angelic mission converging. (ATTESTED — Parry 183–200; Szőnyi; 220 hits.)
- **1584, Cracow**: **Nalvage** and the **Gates of Nature** — 49 governors, 48 keys,
  spelled from tables, delivered explicitly as an instrument for judging the elements
  before the end. (ATTESTED — Harkness 199–213; 63 hits Nalvage, 16 for the Gates.)
- **1584–1586, Prague and Rudolf II.** (ATTESTED — Harkness 20–37; Parry 183–215.) The
  nuncio **Malaspina** and **Bishop Sega** press the heresy suspicion (ATTESTED — 30 hits).
- **1586: the books in the furnace**, and their restoration. (ATTESTED — 193 hits on the
  burning material.) The game does not adjudicate whether the restoration was staged.
- **1586–1589, Třeboň** under **Vilém Rožmberk**. (ATTESTED — Parry 200–226; Harkness
  249–255.)
- **April 1587: the communion of wives.** (ATTESTED — Parry 200–215; Harkness 249–255;
  the timeline's own entry is titled "The communion of wives crisis.") Parry's chapters 16
  and 17 supply the mechanism: the partnership is a *governed enterprise with contested
  authority*, and Dee's authority breaks down precisely as **Kelley gains independent
  value as an alchemist**. The covenant is extorted against the patronage economy, not
  only against belief.
- **The Mortlake library despoiled** during the absence. (ATTESTED — Sherman; 120 hits.)
- **1589: returns to England without Kelley.** (ATTESTED — Parry 226–238; Harkness 249–255.)

## Act VII — The Petition (1589–1605)

- **1592, the *Compendious Rehearsal*** before royal commissioners — the life written out
  as a plea. Parry's ch. 11: **self-defence becomes a central practice** of the later
  career. (ATTESTED — Parry 138–151; 77 hits.)
- **The counter-revolution against magic** as an independent force reshaping his prospects
  (Parry ch. 18, 226–238), sharpened by invasion fear and anti-Spanish panic (ch. 19,
  238–258). (ATTESTED.)
- **1595: Warden of Christ's College, Manchester** — employment *and* marginalisation;
  Parry's chapter is titled "Checkmate: Exiling the Conjuror to Manchester." (ATTESTED —
  Parry 258–273; Harkness 251–257; 143 hits.)
- **The Darrell exorcism controversy**, and Parry's closing argument that later religious
  politics hardened Dee's image as a dangerous conjuror. (ATTESTED — Parry 273–322;
  19 hits Darrell.)
- **1599, the *Apologetical Letter*** — the public self-defence in print. (ATTESTED —
  Sherman.)
- **1604–05: the petition to King James to be tried**, so as to be cleared at law of the
  conjuror slander. **Refused.** (ATTESTED but **thinly** in this corpus — 7 hits on the
  slander/James query; the word *sclaunder* appears twice. The petition itself is a
  well-known document; **before shipping `d35`'s scene text, read Parry ch. 11 and the
  Manchester chapters directly.**) This is the game's thesis beat, so it carries the
  highest verification priority of any single claim in the file.

## Act VIII — The Catalogue (1605–1609)

- **Jane Dee dies**, plague, Manchester, 1605. (CONTEXT; check Fenton's day books.)
- **1607: late angelic actions** with **Bartholomew Hickman**. (ATTESTED — Harkness
  249–261; 63 hits Hickman.) **Francis Nichols** and the discord between scryers
  (ATTESTED — 18 hits).
- **Selling the books**; **Katherine Dee** keeping the house. (ATTESTED — 49 hits
  Katherine; Sherman on the library's dispersal.)
- **Death**, 1608 or 1609; register lost. (ATTESTED — Harkness 249–267.)
- **1659: Casaubon publishes *A True & Faithful Relation*** — printing the conversations
  in order to discredit them, and preserving them permanently. (ATTESTED — Harkness
  251–267; Szőnyi ch. 8, 241–281; **574 hits**, the densest single topic in the corpus.)
  **Ashmole** collects the relics (ATTESTED — 291 hits). Szőnyi's argument: Dee's meaning
  is *produced* by this community of patrons, readers, polemicists and editors — the
  reception is part of the object.

## The Ottoman route (COUNTERFACTUAL — M-K 2021)

The single licensed departure from the record.

- **The record**: Dee's imperial bids ran through London and Prague, both failed; he never
  went east; he died poor with the library dispersed.
- **The counterfactual**: Constantinople, and the court of **Murad III** (r. 1574–95) —
  Sufi initiate, devoted to oneiromancy, astrology, physiognomy and talismanry;
  self-fashioned as prophet-saint-king on the strength of personal spiritual
  communications *paralleling Dee's own*; his reign spanning the Islamic millennium
  (992 AH / 1592 CE). A court where Dee's exact profile was patronage-worthy rather than
  prosecutable.
- **In game**: cultivable from Act I onward via the `ottoman` quantity; surfaces as node
  `o1`; near-miss is the default; full arrival is the rare earned ending, played under the
  COUNTERFACTUAL mark with an epilogue naming the real record.

## Traps in the sources

**There are two Jane Dees.** Dee's **mother, Jane Dee**, died at Mortlake at four in the
morning, aged 77 — the day book records that the Queen came from Court with her train an
hour later and called at his door. Dee's **wife, Jane Fromond**, married 1578, died of
plague at Manchester in 1605. A day-book entry naming "Jane Dee" in the 1570s or 1580 is
almost certainly the mother. **Check which Jane before writing any scene that turns on
her.** (ATTESTED — day books, p. 24.)

**"E.K." is Kelley, and the diaries mostly call him that.** Searching the corpus for
"Kelley" misses a great deal; search `E.K.` as well.

**The private day books and the *Mysteriorum libri* are different records.** The day book
is domestic and terse; the *libri* are the formal action-record. The same day can appear
in both, differently. `docs/DAYBOOK_TEXTURE.md` mines the former;
`RESEARCH_INDEX.md` §2 indexes the latter.
